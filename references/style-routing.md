# Style routing from reference images

Use this guide when the user wants style suggestions, asks the skill to choose a fitting style, or supplies identity images without a selected art direction. The goal is an original design matched to visible traits—not a replica of an existing collectible character.

## 1. Separate identity from presentation

For every reference, record two layers:

- **Identity evidence:** silhouette, face or object geometry, markings, proportions, asymmetry, appendages, and recognition-critical details.
- **Presentation evidence:** pose, lighting, clothing, background, mood, lens distortion, grooming, and temporary accessories.

Do not let a costume, filter, facial expression, or photographic setting overwrite stable identity. For a person, animal, or personal photo, do not infer temperament, intelligence, health, ethnicity, gender identity, or other sensitive traits. Match only visible design features and the mood explicitly shown or requested.

## 2. Build a visual profile

Rate each dimension as low, medium, or high and keep a one-line evidence note:

| Dimension | Low end | High end |
|---|---|---|
| Silhouette energy | round, compact, stable | angular, lanky, asymmetric |
| Emotional temperature | calm, warm, open | cool, guarded, intense |
| Surface language | soft, matte, plush | glossy, metallic, translucent |
| Detail density | iconic, minimal | layered, ornamental, narrative |
| Color energy | muted, earthy, milky | saturated, contrasting, neon |
| Age coding | baby-like, dependent | youthful, self-possessed |
| Reality mode | everyday, domestic | dreamlike, theatrical, mythic |
| Comic energy | sincere, gentle | mischievous, awkward, deadpan |

Also record hard constraints: markings that must survive, anatomy that cannot be simplified, colors that must remain recognizable, and features that would become offensive or misleading if exaggerated.

## 3. Original style families

These families abstract useful visual principles from contemporary collectible-toy design. Use the descriptive family name in prompts; never use a company, artist, or existing IP name as the requested style.

### Classic fashion mascot

- **Language:** large clean head, compact body, confident neutral face, crisp outfit shapes.
- **Palette/material:** bright controlled colors, smooth vinyl, optional metallic or transparent accent.
- **Best fit:** references with a strong face, tidy geometry, distinctive hairstyle or accessory, and confident posture.
- **Sticker strength:** greetings, reactions, daily outfits, collaboration themes.
- **Avoid:** copying a famous pout, eye shape, hairstyle, or astronaut shell.

### Odd-cute forest imp

- **Language:** pointed or leaf-like silhouette, oversized ears or hood shapes, compact furry body, playful open expression.
- **Palette/material:** woodland neutrals or candy accents; plush body with a clean face plate.
- **Best fit:** energetic pets, subjects with expressive ears, unruly fur, mischievous poses, or outdoor cues.
- **Sticker strength:** teasing, chaos, celebration, food stealing, energetic motion.
- **Avoid:** signature tooth counts, proprietary ear shapes, or recognizable monster faces.

### Dark editorial shapeshifter

- **Language:** sculptural head framing, closed or restrained expression, fashion-forward layering, controlled asymmetry.
- **Palette/material:** black, bone, smoke, muted jewel tones; pearlescent, translucent, ceramic, or matte finishes.
- **Best fit:** high-contrast references, elegant long lines, composed poses, unusual headwear, or a cool requested mood.
- **Sticker strength:** deadpan reactions, refusal, confidence, night themes.
- **Avoid:** skull motifs or signature headgear unless independently motivated by the subject.

### Soft dream companion

- **Language:** round childlike proportions, cloudlike or floating secondary shapes, gentle eyes, nature companions.
- **Palette/material:** mist blue, lilac, milk white, translucent glow, soft matte vinyl.
- **Best fit:** pale or fluffy subjects, rounded facial structure, sleepy expression, soft lighting, sky/water/nature cues.
- **Sticker strength:** sleep, comfort, waiting, encouragement, daydreaming.
- **Avoid:** adding a recognizable cloud hairstyle; derive the floating motif from the subject.

### Worn-story wanderer

- **Language:** small guarded figure, lowered gaze, repaired garments, found-object props, tactile imperfections.
- **Palette/material:** grey-green, rust, brown, faded red; worn cloth, paper, clay, scuffed surfaces.
- **Best fit:** asymmetrical markings, weathered objects, reserved poses, documentary photos, or a reflective theme requested by the user.
- **Sticker strength:** apology, fatigue, solitude, resilience, quietly comic struggle.
- **Avoid:** defaulting a real person's neutral face to sadness; use only shown or requested mood.

### Tearful emotional pop

- **Language:** round face, oversized readable emotion symbol, soft hair mass, direct front-facing reactions.
- **Palette/material:** pink, red, blue, glossy highlights, transparent droplets or bubbles.
- **Best fit:** highly expressive faces, round eyes, strong cheek shapes, or an explicitly emotional/cathartic brief.
- **Sticker strength:** crying, gratitude, affection, frustration, dramatic pleading.
- **Avoid:** making tears the permanent identity anchor; vary emotion symbols and preserve originality.

### Storybook nature sprite

- **Language:** baby-like body, small fantasy appendages derived from plants/animals, handcrafted storybook detail.
- **Palette/material:** botanical pastels, cream, moss, pollen yellow; matte clay or softly painted vinyl.
- **Best fit:** small animals, plants, rounded objects, gentle subjects, natural textures, folk or garden settings.
- **Sticker strength:** luck, gifting, seasonal greetings, cooking, tiny adventures.
- **Avoid:** generic fantasy clutter; choose one natural motif tied to the reference.

### Warm homebody plush

- **Language:** chubby proportions, enveloping costume, low center of gravity, hug-friendly silhouette.
- **Palette/material:** milk tea, cream, peach, powder colors; flocking, plush, knitted accents.
- **Best fit:** round pets, soft fur, sleepy or food-related photos, domestic objects, affectionate briefs.
- **Sticker strength:** eating, hugging, resting, acknowledgement, everyday chat.
- **Avoid:** removing distinctive markings under a costume.

### Youth street rebel

- **Language:** slightly longer limbs, bold stance, sneakers or functional streetwear, graphic prop family.
- **Palette/material:** black with red/blue/neon accents, denim, rubber, matte plastic.
- **Best fit:** active poses, lean silhouettes, sports/music cues, angular features, energetic requested mood.
- **Sticker strength:** cheering, refusal, departure, teamwork, hype reactions.
- **Avoid:** unlicensed logos, band identities, and trend details that obscure the subject.

### Punk ensemble cartoon

- **Language:** expressive elongated figures, differentiated personalities, exaggerated hair and performance gestures.
- **Palette/material:** plaid, leather-like matte surfaces, fluorescent accents, graphic illustration finish.
- **Best fit:** multiple subjects, friend groups, music references, strong individual styling.
- **Sticker strength:** group banter, role-based reactions, performance and friendship themes.
- **Avoid:** forcing a solo subject into a fixed trio or copying recognizable band-character designs.

### Starry comfort icon

- **Language:** extremely simple rounded body, radiating or soft geometric outer silhouette, tiny facial features.
- **Palette/material:** warm yellow, cream, pale blue and pink; plush or velvety finish.
- **Best fit:** simple objects, very round animals, minimal features, bedtime or encouragement themes.
- **Sticker strength:** good night, wishes, support, love, subtle animation.
- **Avoid:** replacing identity with a generic star; integrate the subject's silhouette into the geometry.

### Candy-gel translucent

- **Language:** rounded simplified anatomy, juicy volume, embedded or suspended micro-details.
- **Palette/material:** transparent candy colors, gradients, glossy gel, luminous edges.
- **Best fit:** glass, water, fruit, glossy products, bright smooth subjects, playful food themes.
- **Sticker strength:** delight, surprise, refreshment, summer, small looping motions.
- **Avoid:** using translucency where markings or facial contrast would disappear.

## 4. Match and rank

For each candidate family, score 0–2 on:

1. silhouette compatibility;
2. identity-detail survival;
3. emotional fit with shown/requested mood;
4. material plausibility;
5. sticker readability at 120 px;
6. expression range across a full pack.

Subtract 2 for each major conflict, such as hidden recognition markings, incompatible anatomy, a style that depends on an unrequested negative mood, or a surface treatment that erases contrast.

Choose:

- **Lead:** highest score and no major identity conflict.
- **Runner-up:** within 2 points of the lead or offering a useful contrasting emotional temperature.
- **Wild card:** optional only when the user requested broad exploration; it must still preserve identity.

Do not present numerical scores unless they help the user decide. Normally summarize the evidence in plain language.

## 5. Combine conservatively

One family should control silhouette and proportion. A second may contribute only one supporting axis: material, palette, or emotional temperature. Do not blend more than two families in the first concept round.

Useful combinations include:

- soft dream companion + candy-gel material;
- warm homebody plush + storybook nature motif;
- dark editorial silhouette + youth street palette;
- worn-story narrative + soft dream emotional warmth;
- odd-cute silhouette + homebody plush material.

If the combination weakens thumbnail recognition, return to a single family.

## 6. Output a compact recommendation

Before concept generation, provide:

```text
Visible anchors: [3–5 identity traits]
Lead direction: [family] — [visible evidence and expected benefit]
Runner-up: [family] — [meaningful alternative]
Keep fixed: [identity invariants]
Avoid: [identity risks and source-IP signatures]
```

Then translate the selected family into concrete prompt attributes: proportions, silhouette, expression grammar, palette, materials, lighting, and forbidden changes. Keep prompts descriptive and source-neutral.
