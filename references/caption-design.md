# Caption design

## Selection principles

- Prefer 2–6 Chinese characters; split longer copy into two balanced lines.
- Make the pose understandable without copy and let the copy sharpen the joke.
- Mix about two-thirds durable chat utility with one-third current phrases.
- Preserve the character voice across the whole set: deadpan, warm, chaotic, proud, or restrained.
- Research phrases the user calls “current,” “hot,” or “trending.” Cite the sources in the final handoff.

## Safety and originality

- Avoid direct movie/TV lines, lyrics, celebrity catchphrases, branded slogans, and another character's named meme.
- Avoid text that implies official endorsement, certification, or authenticity.
- Screen gambling, violence, harassment, discriminatory language, and sexually suggestive phrases before submission.
- Prefer semantic remixes and ordinary speech over copied quotations.

## Action-copy fit

| Action | Copy pattern |
| --- | --- |
| wave or entrance | greeting, arrival, “来都来了” |
| paw/hand to chest | acknowledgement, confidence |
| bow | thanks or apology |
| belly-up flailing | laughter or losing composure |
| side-eye loaf | speechless, resignation |
| extreme head tilt | question or disbelief |
| crossed limbs | refusal or deflection |
| flattened body | fatigue, lying flat, barely functioning |
| sleeping curl | good night, self-care |
| moving away and looking back | leaving, retreating, awkward exit |

## Typography

- Generate visuals without text; typeset exact copy afterward.
- Use a strong fill, dark inner outline, and light outer rim for light/dark chat backgrounds.
- Keep line spacing tight and avoid thin strokes.
- Test at 240 px and 120 px, not only on the master.
